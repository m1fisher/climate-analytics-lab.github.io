  <h1>MOSAIC</h1>
  <p>Multi-scale Observation-driven Symbolic AI for Climate</p>
</div>

<p class="mosaic-intro">MOSAIC is building an AI-assisted path from real-world evidence to better climate-model equations. It brings observational data, symbolic reasoning, differentiable simulation, and rigorous evaluation into one research loop—helping scientists propose interpretable parameterizations, test them in a global model, and learn from every result.</p>

## Why this matters

Many of the processes that shape climate projections—clouds, rain formation, aerosol activation, land-surface exchange, and boundary-layer mixing—occur at scales too fine for a global model to resolve directly. Scientists represent their aggregate effects with *parameterizations*: compact equations that have traditionally taken years of expert design and hand-tuning.

At the same time, satellites, field campaigns, reanalyses, and high-resolution simulations contain far more process-level information than researchers can manually distill. MOSAIC asks whether an AI system can help turn that evidence into useful scientific hypotheses: **Can it derive new, physically credible equations, insert them into a global atmospheric model, and calibrate them against observations to improve on hand-tuned baselines?**

The aim is not to replace physical understanding with a black box. It is to expand the scientist's toolkit while retaining analytic equations that can be inspected, challenged, and improved.

## The research loop

<div class="mosaic-step-grid">
  <div class="mosaic-step">
    <span class="mosaic-step__number">01</span>
    <h3>Curate the evidence</h3>
    <p>The agent works with matched satellite, reanalysis, field-campaign, and high-resolution simulation data. Initial sources include NASA MODIS, DOE Atmospheric Radiation Measurement data, ERA5, LASSO, and ClimSim.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mosaic-step">
    <span class="mosaic-step__number">02</span>
    <h3>Discover candidate equations</h3>
    <p>Physics-guided agents formulate hypotheses and orchestrate symbolic-regression and symmetry-discovery tools. Candidates are screened for dimensions, conservation, boundedness, stability, and interpretability.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mosaic-step">
    <span class="mosaic-step__number">03</span>
    <h3>Integrate and calibrate</h3>
    <p>Promising equations become modular, differentiable JAX components. They are inserted into JAX-GCM and tuned through model rollouts against observable quantities—not just against an isolated training dataset.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mosaic-step">
    <span class="mosaic-step__number">04</span>
    <h3>Evaluate and learn</h3>
    <p>Each candidate is compared with existing parameterizations on withheld observations, unfamiliar weather regimes, and longer global simulations. Diagnostics and failures become evidence for the next discovery cycle.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## What makes MOSAIC possible

MOSAIC combines recent advances that are more powerful together than in isolation:

- **A differentiable climate model.** [JAX-GCM (JCM)](/projects/jaxgcm/) is an open, intermediate-complexity atmospheric model written in JAX. Automatic differentiation lets us tune a candidate parameterization through the model against observations and investigate how uncertain processes affect downstream climate metrics. Explore the [code on GitHub](https://github.com/climate-analytics-lab/jax-gcm) or read the [JCM v1.1 paper](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-19-6451-2026).
- **Physics-guided AI agents.** [KeplerAgent](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.12259) provides a foundation for coordinating symbolic regression and symmetry discovery while reasoning about governing equations and physical constraints.
- **A demonstrated scientific starting point.** Earlier work showed that equation discovery and automatic tuning can reduce cloud-cover errors in a hybrid climate model. MOSAIC expands that idea from one component into a repeatable, agent-assisted workflow spanning multiple processes.
- **Evaluation at the scale that matters.** Candidate equations must do more than fit local training data. We will test them in global simulations, against withheld observations, and across regimes and initial conditions—measuring predictive skill, stability, and physical consistency together.

### A broader path to observation-driven Earth-system modeling

The initial program concentrates on demonstrating a rigorous end-to-end atmospheric workflow. From there, the same approach can extend into differentiable land and coupled Earth-system modeling. Because JAX-GCM exposes model gradients, MOSAIC can ultimately ask a second question: *which additional observation would most reduce the uncertainty in a climate prediction?* Those sensitivity maps could guide future satellite measurements, field campaigns, and targeted ocean observations—including turbulence-equipped Argo floats.
